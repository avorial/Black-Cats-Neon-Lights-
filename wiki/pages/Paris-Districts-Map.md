# Guide to the Arrondissements

![Paris Districts](/wiki/media/Paris Districts.jpg)

## Overview

The Rex Flavus arrondissement guide turns Paris into a playable investigation map. Each district carries a social mood, a few recognizable landmarks, and at least one Yellow King or Carcosan pressure point. The large map is preserved in the campaign media library as `Paris Districts.jpg`.

## District Bands

- Royal Heart, 1-4: old power, government, ancient money, and secrets under civic stone.
- Student's Left Bank, 5-7: art students, radical philosophy, publishing, military monuments, and psychic weather around the Eiffel Tower.
- Wealthy Right Bank, 8-9: polite wealth, occult indulgence, velvet decadence, and the [[Palais Garnier]].
- Industrial Gateways, 10-12: stations, fog, workers, anarchists, rail movement, and strange arrivals.
- Outer Ring, 13-20: poisoned water, catacombs, slaughterhouses, old wealth, rail smoke, Montmartre, artificial landscapes, and the restless dead.

## Arrondissement Notes

### 1. Louvre - The Palace

Imperial, watched, and full of stolen sacred objects. The Louvre is a maze of old gods and imperial loot; statues may move when guards are alone. The Tuileries are precise and manicured, but the birds and shadows suggest something older than the Republic is listening.

### 2. Bourse - The Market

Frenetic, greedy, and desperate. The Stock Exchange is where fortunes and sanity are lost. Passage des Panoramas makes a good chase route, ambush site, or impossible shopfront where time behaves poorly.

### 3. Temple - The Quiet Quarter

Medieval, silent, and alchemical. The Square du Temple overlays Templar prison ruins, while the National Archives hold records of bloodlines and secrets that should not fit human history.

### 4. Hotel-de-Ville - The Historic Center

Gothic civic dread. Notre-Dame's gargoyles are increasingly restless, and Ile Saint-Louis shelters aristocrats who trust the river to ward off the Yellow Sign.

### 5. Pantheon - The Latin Quarter

Likely player hub territory for art students. The Sorbonne has books outside the curriculum, cheap wine and revolutionary arguments fill the streets, and the Pantheon becomes tomb-like and oversized after dark.

### 6. Luxembourg - The Garden

Romantic but melancholy. Jardin du Luxembourg is beautiful in daylight, but its statues cast wrong shadows at twilight. The local publishers help spread avant-garde writing that may be driving young artists mad.

### 7. Palais-Bourbon - The Iron Needle

Monumental and military. The Eiffel Tower acts as a hated iron lightning rod for psychic energy. Les Invalides keeps Napoleon's tomb, with rumors that the dead emperor is not resting quietly.

### 8. Elysee - The Gilded Cage

Wide boulevards, cold power, and masked depravity. Wealthy occult societies hide behind manners, while the President's palace is protected by guards who ignore the wrong kinds of screams.

### 9. Opera - The Decadence

Electric, hedonistic, and theatrical. The [[Palais Garnier]] dominates with gold, velvet, and the subterranean lake beneath it. The Grand Boulevards are places to buy anything, including memories.

### 10. Entrepot - The Transit Zone

Fog, movement, and transient lives. Gare du Nord and Gare de l'Est serve as twin gates to the city. Canal Saint-Martin is a good place for bodies, disappearances, ecstatic corpses, and evidence that should have sunk.

### 11. Popincourt - The Revolution

Working-class, volatile, and angry. The streets remember barricades. Back-room anarchist cells can be mundane revolutionaries, King-tainted cells, or both.

### 12. Reuilly - The Wood's Edge

Industrial emptiness meets wild ground. Gare de Lyon brings travelers from the South, possibly from stranger routes. Bois de Vincennes is useful for duels, escaped zoo horrors, and disappearances at the edge of respectability.

### 13. Gobelins - The Poisoned River

Chemical smells and isolation. The covered Bievre carries dyes, tannery waste, and color changes that can be played as omen or contamination. Butte-aux-Cailles isolates itself from the rest of Paris.

### 14. Observatory - The City of Bones

Hollow ground and celestial obsession. The Catacombs entrance is here, bones arrange themselves into patterns, and astronomers are tracking a new star in the Hyades.

### 15. Vaugirard - The Slaughterhouse

Blood, copper, and industrial meat. The abattoirs process the city's flesh, while the Pasteur Institute can tilt from biology into necrology or forbidden medical research.

### 16. Passy - The Silent Wealth

High walls, old money, and paranoia. The rich board windows and guard estates against a color or presence they cannot fully name.

### 17. Batignolles - The Railway Web

Steam, noise, confusion, and rails cutting the neighborhood apart. It is useful for fugitives, surveillance breaks, handoffs, and people disappearing into smoke.

### 18. Montmartre - The Artist's Asylum

High danger zone and epicenter of artistic madness. Sacre-Coeur is still under construction and its foundations crack. The Moulin Rouge and cabarets can be played as rituals disguised as nightlife.

### 19. Buttes-Chaumont - The Artificial Park

Surreal, staged, and wrong. The park's cliffs, caves, and bridges feel built like scenery. The bridge is tied to readers or viewers of The King in Yellow who have lost the ability to remain in ordinary Paris.

### 20. Menilmontant - The Necropolis

Quiet, crowded with the dead, and centered on Pere Lachaise. The cemetery is a city inside the city, and mausoleums are sometimes found opened from the inside.

## GM Use

- Start with the district band to set mood, then use the individual arrondissement hook for the scene complication.
- Use the map for route planning, surveillance, cab routes, police jurisdictions, and pursuit clocks.
- Treat each district as having a mundane Paris explanation and a King-tainted explanation; let clues decide which one is true.
